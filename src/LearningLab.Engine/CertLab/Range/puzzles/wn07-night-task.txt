@intro
You are on a pretend Windows computer. Windows' Task Scheduler runs programs on a timer,
and attackers like to hide there among the many real tasks. Someone added a task that
runs a PowerShell script every night at {{HOUR}} a.m. and passes it a secret token on its
command line. Give that token.

@winsetup
New-Item -ItemType Directory -Path 'C:\ProgramData\{{TASK}}' -Force | Out-Null
Set-Content -Path 'C:\ProgramData\{{TASK}}\run.ps1' -Value '# nightly helper'
$a = New-ScheduledTaskAction -Execute 'powershell.exe' -Argument '-NoProfile -WindowStyle Hidden -File C:\ProgramData\{{TASK}}\run.ps1 -Token {{TOKEN}}'
$t = New-ScheduledTaskTrigger -Daily -At '{{HOUR}}:00'
Register-ScheduledTask -TaskName '{{TASK}}' -TaskPath '\' -Action $a -Trigger $t -User 'SYSTEM' | Out-Null

@solution
Get-ScheduledTask -TaskPath '\' | Select-Object TaskName, State
(Get-ScheduledTask -TaskName '{{TASK}}').Actions | Format-List Execute, Arguments

@walkthrough
Get-ScheduledTask lists every task; most live in folders under \Microsoft\Windows\.
The ones in the top folder (\) are usually added by programs or people:

    Get-ScheduledTask -TaskPath '\' | Select-Object TaskName, State

{{TASK}} sounds like a normal helper, but its action is what matters:

    (Get-ScheduledTask -TaskName '{{TASK}}').Actions | Format-List Execute, Arguments

It starts hidden PowerShell with a script in C:\ProgramData and -Token {{TOKEN}}. A task
is a favourite way to survive restarts; defenders check each task's Actions, not its
name.

@hint 5
Get-ScheduledTask lists the scheduled tasks. Tasks added by people or programs usually sit in the top folder, TaskPath \.

@hint 10
Names can lie. Look at what a suspicious task runs: (Get-ScheduledTask -TaskName NAME).Actions

@hint 15
(Get-ScheduledTask -TaskName '{{TASK}}').Actions | Format-List Execute, Arguments - the value after -Token is the answer.
