@intro
~/mirror.txt holds a word written backwards. Read it the right way round.

@setup
printf '%s' '{{SECRET}}' | rev > /home/player/mirror.txt
chown player: /home/player/mirror.txt

@solution
cat mirror.txt
echo
rev mirror.txt

@walkthrough
rev prints each line with its characters in the opposite order, so running it on a
backwards word gives the word:

    rev mirror.txt

@hint 2
What to think about first: nothing is encoded or hidden here - the letters are just in the opposite order. Look at the file (cat mirror.txt) and try reading it from right to left in your head.

@hint 3
Reversing text is one of the simplest ways people "hide" a word from a quick glance. It fools nobody who looks closely, but it is a good warm-up for spotting patterns: if a jumble of letters looks almost like a word, try it backwards.

@hint 4
Linux has a tiny tool for exactly this: rev. It reads each line and prints it with the characters in reverse order. It works on a file name or on text piped into it (echo hello | rev prints olleh).

@hint 6
Run: rev mirror.txt
It prints the word the right way round. That word is the answer.
