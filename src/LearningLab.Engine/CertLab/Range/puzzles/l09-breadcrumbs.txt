@intro
Someone hid a flag on this box and left you only one clue: it lives in your
environment.

@setup
groupadd {{GRP}}
usermod -aG {{GRP}} player
mkdir -p /srv/{{GRP}}/.{{HID}}
printf '%s' '{{XOR:K:FLAG}}' | base32 > /srv/{{GRP}}/.{{HID}}/data.txt
printf 'second lock: one byte, %s\n' '{{K}}' > /srv/{{GRP}}/.{{HID}}/key.txt
chgrp -R {{GRP}} /srv/{{GRP}}
chmod 750 /srv/{{GRP}} /srv/{{GRP}}/.{{HID}}
chmod 640 /srv/{{GRP}}/.{{HID}}/data.txt /srv/{{GRP}}/.{{HID}}/key.txt
cat >> /home/player/.bashrc <<'RC'
export NEXT_STOP=/srv/{{GRP}}/.{{HID}}
RC

@solution
printenv | grep -i next
ls -la /srv/{{GRP}}/.{{HID}}
cat /srv/{{GRP}}/.{{HID}}/key.txt
base32 -d /srv/{{GRP}}/.{{HID}}/data.txt > /tmp/step.hex
xorcat -k {{K}} /tmp/step.hex

@walkthrough
1. printenv shows NEXT_STOP=/srv/{{GRP}}/.{{HID}}, a hidden folder.
2. It belongs to the group {{GRP}} (mode 750), and id shows you are in it, so you may
   read it.
3. data.txt is base32 (only A-Z, 2-7 and =): base32 -d gives hex digits.
4. key.txt says the second lock is one byte, {{K}}: XOR. xorcat -k {{K}} on the hex
   prints the flag. (xorcat --brute would also find it.)

Each step on its own is easy; chaining them is the skill.

@hint 25
printenv, then id: the folder's group is one of yours. data.txt is base32; what's inside is hex for xorcat.
