@intro
An audit rule says nobody may be in both the {{G1}} group (who approve payments) and the
{{G2}} group (who run the servers): one person with both could pay themselves and hide
it. Exactly one account breaks the rule. Who?

@setup
for u in {{U1}} {{U2}} {{U3}} {{U4}} {{U5}}; do useradd -m $u; done
groupadd {{G1}}
groupadd {{G2}}
groupadd staff2
usermod -aG {{G1}} {{U2}}
usermod -aG {{G1}} {{U3}}
usermod -aG {{G1}},staff2 {{U1}}
usermod -aG {{G2}} {{U4}}
usermod -aG {{G2}},staff2 {{U5}}
usermod -aG {{G2}} {{U1}}

@solution
getent group {{G1}}
getent group {{G2}}

@walkthrough
/etc/group lists each group with its members after the last colon. getent group NAME
prints one group's line:

    getent group {{G1}}   -> {{G1}}:x:...:{{U2}},{{U3}},{{U1}}
    getent group {{G2}}   -> {{G2}}:x:...:{{U4}},{{U5}},{{U1}}

The name in both lists is {{U1}}. (id {{U1}} also shows both groups.) Separation of
duties like this is a common audit check; the fix is removing one membership
(gpasswd -d {{U1}} {{G2}}).

@hint 10
Group members are listed in /etc/group, after the last colon of each group's line.

@hint 20
getent group {{G1}} and getent group {{G2}}: the name that appears in both is the answer.
