@intro
You are step15. passwords.old and passwords.new are two lists of 200 codes each. They
are identical except for a single line that was changed. The password for step 16 is
the changed line in passwords.new.

@setup
useradd -m step16
echo 'step16:{{PW}}' | chpasswd
for i in $(seq 1 200); do printf '%s\n' "{{SALT}}/$i" | md5sum | cut -c1-10; done > /root/base.txt
(head -n $(({{L}} - 1)) /root/base.txt; printf '%s\n' '{{OLD}}'; tail -n +$(({{L}} + 1)) /root/base.txt) > /home/step15/passwords.old
(head -n $(({{L}} - 1)) /root/base.txt; printf '%s\n' '{{PW}}'; tail -n +$(({{L}} + 1)) /root/base.txt) > /home/step15/passwords.new
rm -f /root/base.txt
chown step15: /home/step15/passwords.old /home/step15/passwords.new

@solution
wc -l passwords.old passwords.new
diff passwords.old passwords.new

@walkthrough
diff compares two files line by line and prints only what differs:

    diff passwords.old passwords.new

The output reads like "{{L}}c{{L}}" (line {{L}} was changed), then "< " and the line
from the first file, "---", and "> " and the line from the second file. The line after
">" is the new password. People use diff every day to see what changed between two
versions of a settings file or a program.

@hint 10
Comparing 200 lines by eye is slow and easy to get wrong. The command diff takes two files and prints only the lines that are different.

@hint 18
Type: diff passwords.old passwords.new - the line starting with > comes from the second file, passwords.new. That code is the answer.
