@intro
A malware sample's configuration was saved as ~/{{NAME}}.hex. Analysts say it is
"encrypted" with a single-byte XOR, but nobody knows the key.

@setup
printf '%s\n' '{{XOR:K:FLAG}}' > /home/player/{{NAME}}.hex
chown player: /home/player/{{NAME}}.hex

@solution
cat {{NAME}}.hex
xorcat --brute {{NAME}}.hex

@walkthrough
One byte has only 255 useful values, so try them all and keep the results that look
like plain text:

    xorcat --brute {{NAME}}.hex

Only a few keys give printable text, and one of them (key {{K}}) reads FLAG{...}. Real
malware often uses a single-byte XOR to hide strings from a quick look; analysts break
it exactly like this.

@hint 15
A single byte can only be 1 to 255. Trying every key is quick - and xorcat can do it for you (man xorcat).

@hint 25
xorcat --brute {{NAME}}.hex - look for the line that reads FLAG{...}.
