@intro
~/shadow.bak is a backup of a password file: each line is USER:HASH:... The hash
starts with $6$, the sha512-crypt scheme. hashcrack can try the word list against it.
What is the user "sam" password?

@setup
useradd -m sam
echo 'sam:{{SECRET}}' | chpasswd
grep '^sam:' /etc/shadow > /home/player/shadow.bak
chown player: /home/player/shadow.bak
chmod 644 /home/player/shadow.bak

@solution
cat shadow.bak
hashcrack shadow.bak

@walkthrough
The $6$ at the start marks a sha512-crypt hash, as used in /etc/shadow. hashcrack
recognises it and tries each word of the list:

    hashcrack shadow.bak

The lesson: a readable backup of /etc/shadow is as good as handing over every weak
password on the machine. Keep shadow backups to root only.

@hint 5
A $6$ hash is salted and slow on purpose, but a weak password is still weak: guessing likely words and comparing still works.

@hint 10
Just run hashcrack on the file: hashcrack shadow.bak (it recognises the $6$ lines by itself).

@hint 15
The password is a common word; hashcrack shadow.bak finds it from the built-in list, and hashcrack --show shadow.bak shows it again next to sam's name.
