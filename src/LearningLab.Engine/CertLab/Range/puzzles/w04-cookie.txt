@intro
An internal page at http://localhost/panel decides whether you are an administrator by
reading a cookie your browser sends - which means your browser gets to decide. Set the
right cookie and the admin panel (with the flag) opens.

@setup
systemctl enable --now httpd

@web
[
  { "path": "/panel", "kind": "cookie", "title": "Admin panel", "cookie": "{{ROLE}}={{ADMIN}}",
    "body": "<h1>Members area</h1><p>You are a normal member. Administrators see more here.</p>",
    "template": "<h1>Admin panel</h1><p>Welcome, administrator.</p><p>flag: {{FLAG}}</p>" }
]

@solution
curl -si http://localhost/panel
curl -s -b "{{ROLE}}={{ADMIN}}" http://localhost/panel

@walkthrough
First load the page and look at the headers and body:

    curl -si http://localhost/panel

As a normal visitor you only see the members area, but the page trusts a cookie to
decide if you are an admin. You can send any cookie you like with -b:

    curl -s -b "{{ROLE}}={{ADMIN}}" http://localhost/panel

and the admin panel opens with the flag. The lesson: never trust the client to tell
you who it is. Roles belong in a signed session the server controls, not in a plain
cookie the browser can set.

@hint 6
Anything your browser sends - addresses, form fields, cookies - is under your control. If a page decides who you are from something you send, you can change what it believes.

@hint 12
Look at what the page reads to decide you are an admin: a cookie (curl -si shows the headers). curl sends a cookie
with -b "name=value".

@hint 22
The cookie it trusts is {{ROLE}}={{ADMIN}}. Send it:
curl -s -b "{{ROLE}}={{ADMIN}}" http://localhost/panel
