teaser: a Vigenere message and a sticky note

answer: flag
var: KW = word
var: NOTE = pick:sticky.txt|todo.txt|reminder.txt|desk-note.txt

@intro
~/secret.txt looks like a flag, but its letters are wrong: it was put through a
Vigenere cipher with a key word. The owner keeps their key words on ~/{{NOTE}} - in a
form they thought nobody would read.

@setup
printf '%s\n' '{{VIG:KW:FLAG}}' > /home/player/secret.txt
printf 'groceries: milk, eggs\nkey word (rot13, so nobody reads it): {{ROT13:KW}}\ncall the plumber\n' > /home/player/{{NOTE}}
chown player: /home/player/secret.txt /home/player/{{NOTE}}

@solution
cat {{NOTE}}
echo {{ROT13:KW}} | tr 'A-Za-z' 'N-ZA-Mn-za-m'
vigenere -d -k {{KW}} secret.txt

@walkthrough
1. The note's key word is ROT13'd: every letter moved 13 places, so doing it again
   undoes it: echo {{ROT13:KW}} | tr 'A-Za-z' 'N-ZA-Mn-za-m' gives {{KW}}.
2. Vigenere moves each letter by the matching key-word letter. Decrypt with the key:

       vigenere -d -k {{KW}} secret.txt

   prints the flag (punctuation and digits pass through unchanged, which is why it
   still looked like a flag).

The lesson: a cipher is only as strong as the place its key is kept.

@hint 15
Two steps: the key word on the note is itself hidden with ROT13 (tr can undo it), and vigenere -d uses the key word.

@hint 25
tr 'A-Za-z' 'N-ZA-Mn-za-m' undoes ROT13; then vigenere -d -k WORD secret.txt.
