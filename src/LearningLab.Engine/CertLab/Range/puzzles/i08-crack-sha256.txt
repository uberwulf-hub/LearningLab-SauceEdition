@intro
~/digest.txt holds a password as a SHA-256 hash (64 hex digits). Tell hashcrack it is
sha256 and let it try the word list. What is the password?

@setup
printf 'web:{{SHA256:SECRET}}\n' > /home/player/digest.txt
chown player: /home/player/digest.txt

@solution
cat digest.txt
hashcrack --format=sha256 digest.txt

@walkthrough
A 64-hex-digit hash could be SHA-256. Tell hashcrack which scheme to use:

    hashcrack --format=sha256 digest.txt

It hashes each word with SHA-256 and compares. Longer hashes take no longer to crack
when the password itself is a common word: the password's strength is what matters.

@hint 4
How long is the hash? 32 hex digits is usually MD5, 40 SHA-1, 64 SHA-256. A cracker needs to know which recipe to try.

@hint 8
hashcrack guesses the type from the length, but you can say it outright: hashcrack --format=sha256 digest.txt

@hint 12
Run: hashcrack --format=sha256 digest.txt - the word it prints is the answer.
