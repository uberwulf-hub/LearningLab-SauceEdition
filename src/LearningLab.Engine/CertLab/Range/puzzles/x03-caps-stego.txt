@intro
~/story.txt reads like a normal note, but a few letters in the middle of words are
capital when they shouldn't be. Read those stray capital letters in order: they
spell a word. What is it?

@setup
word='{{SECRET}}'
normals="morning river valley meadow harbor garden willow summer window autumn"
out=""
i=1
while [ $i -le ${#word} ]; do
  ch=$(printf '%s' "$word" | cut -c$i | tr 'a-z' 'A-Z')
  nm=$(printf '%s' "$normals" | cut -d' ' -f$(( (i-1) % 10 + 1 )))
  out="$out $nm we${ch}re"
  i=$((i+1))
done
printf 'down by the%s again.\n' "$out" > /home/player/story.txt
chown player: /home/player/story.txt

@solution
cat story.txt
grep -oE '[A-Z]' story.txt | tr -d '\n'
echo

@walkthrough
Every few words one has a capital letter in the middle where it shouldn't. Pull out
just the capital letters, in order:

    grep -oE '[A-Z]' story.txt | tr -d '\n'

The lesson: changes too small to notice at a glance (odd capitals, extra spaces,
look-alike characters) can carry a hidden message that tools see even when eyes skip
over it.
