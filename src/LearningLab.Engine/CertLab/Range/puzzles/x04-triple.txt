@intro
~/message.txt was put through three reversible steps, one after another: the word was
reversed, then letters were shifted by 13 (ROT13), then the result was base64-encoded.
Undo them in the opposite order to get the word back.

@setup
printf '%s' '{{SECRET}}' | rev | tr 'A-Za-z' 'N-ZA-Mn-za-m' | base64 > /home/player/message.txt
chown player: /home/player/message.txt

@solution
cat message.txt
base64 -d message.txt
base64 -d message.txt | tr 'A-Za-z' 'N-ZA-Mn-za-m'
base64 -d message.txt | tr 'A-Za-z' 'N-ZA-Mn-za-m' | rev
echo

@walkthrough
The steps were applied in this order: reverse, then ROT13, then base64. To undo them,
reverse the order and undo each:

    base64 -d message.txt \
      | tr 'A-Za-z' 'N-ZA-Mn-za-m' \   # undo ROT13 (ROT13 is its own inverse)
      | rev                            # undo the reversal

The word that comes out is the answer. The lesson: stacking reversible encodings only
looks like encryption - each layer peels off with a standard tool, so none of it is a
secret.

@hint 14
base64 was the last step, so undo it first: base64 -d message.txt. The result still looks
scrambled.

@hint 22
Then undo ROT13 with tr 'A-Za-z' 'N-ZA-Mn-za-m', and finally undo the reversal with rev.
