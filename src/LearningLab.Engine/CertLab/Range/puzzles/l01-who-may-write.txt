@intro
The shared folder /srv/drop holds a few files owned by other people. Most of them you
(the user player) can only read - but one was left so that anyone at all can change it.
That is a classic mistake. Which file can you write to? (Give its name, for example
notes.txt.)

@setup
mkdir -p /srv/drop
for f in prices.csv rota.txt menu.txt contacts.csv orders.txt stock.csv; do
  echo "shared file $f" > /srv/drop/$f
  chmod 644 /srv/drop/$f
done
useradd -M -s /sbin/nologin kitchen 2>/dev/null || true
chown kitchen: /srv/drop/menu.txt /srv/drop/stock.csv
chmod 640 /srv/drop/contacts.csv
chmod 666 /srv/drop/{{WFILE}}
chmod 755 /srv/drop

@solution
ls -l /srv/drop
echo {{WFILE}}

@walkthrough
ls -l shows each file's permissions as ten characters, for example -rw-r--r--:

    ls -l /srv/drop

After the first character (- for a file) come three groups of three: what the owner
may do, what the file's group may do, and what everybody else ("others") may do. r is
read, w is write, - is "not allowed". You are not the owner and not in the group of
these files, so the last three letters are the ones that apply to you.

Almost every file ends in r-- (others may read only). {{WFILE}} ends in rw- (-rw-rw-rw-,
mode 666): anyone on the computer may change it. That is the answer. The fix is
chmod 644 (or 664 with a proper group) so only the right people can write.

@hint 2
What to think about first: Linux keeps, for every file, a short list of who may read it and who may change ("write") it. It splits people into three kinds: the file's owner, the members of the file's group, and everybody else. You are "everybody else" for these files.

@hint 3
The command ls lists a folder; ls -l (a small L) lists it "long", with one line per file. The first column of each line, like -rw-r--r--, is the permission list. Run: ls -l /srv/drop

@hint 4
Read that column in three groups of three after the first character: owner (rw-), group (r--), others (r--). r means read, w means write, and - means not allowed. Because you are neither the owner nor in the group, only the last three letters count for you.

@hint 6
Look for the one line whose last three letters are rw- instead of r-- or ---. It looks like -rw-rw-rw- (the number 666). The name at the end of that line is the answer.
