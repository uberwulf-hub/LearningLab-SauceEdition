@vars TARGET=payroll.xlsx
DECOY = invoices.csv
@vars TARGET=invoices.csv

@vars TARGET=vendors.txt
DECOY = bank-details.txt
@vars TARGET=bank-details.txt
DECOY = payroll.xlsx

@intro
The firewall recorded a stranger's session writing to the finance share at exactly
09:30:{{SEC}} on 14 February 2026. The share was copied to ~/finance with its times kept.
Which file did the stranger change? (Give its name.)

@setup
mkdir -p /home/player/finance
for f in payroll.xlsx invoices.csv vendors.txt bank-details.txt budget.ods; do
  echo "data for $f" > /home/player/finance/$f
  touch -m -d '2026-02-13 16:45:00' /home/player/finance/$f
done
touch -m -d '2026-02-14 09:30:{{OTHER}}' /home/player/finance/{{DECOY}}
touch -m -d '2026-02-14 09:30:{{SEC}}' /home/player/finance/{{TARGET}}
chown -R player: /home/player/finance

@solution
ls -l finance
stat -c '%y %n' finance/*

@walkthrough
ls -l shows modification times only to the minute, and two files say Feb 14 09:30. To
tell them apart you need the seconds, which stat shows:

    stat -c '%y %n' finance/*

(%y is the modification time, %n the name.) {{DECOY}} changed at 09:30:{{OTHER}};
{{TARGET}} at 09:30:{{SEC}} - the firewall's time. Matching events from different sources
by their exact times is the core of building a timeline.

@hint 5
ls -l shows the time only to the minute. Two files fit the minute; you need the seconds.

@hint 10
stat FILE prints the full times, down to fractions of a second. stat -c '%y %n' finance/* prints one line per file.

@hint 15
stat -c '%y %n' finance/* - the file modified at 09:30:{{SEC}} is the answer.
