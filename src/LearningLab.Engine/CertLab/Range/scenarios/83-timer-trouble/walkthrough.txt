Two ways to stay in: a sudo rule and a systemd timer.

1. The log. grep -E 'Accepted|Failed|sudo' /var/log/secure: three failed passwords for {{DEV}} from
   {{ATKIP}}, then an accepted one. Two minutes later {{DEV}} used sudo to run tee into
   /etc/sudoers.d/{{SUDOF}}, and then systemctl enable --now {{TIMER}}.timer.

2. The sudo rule. /etc/sudoers.d/{{SUDOF}} says "{{DEV}} ALL=(ALL) NOPASSWD: ALL": that account may now
   run anything as root without even typing a password. The file name was chosen to look harmless.

3. The timer. systemctl list-timers --all shows {{TIMER}}.timer ("{{TIMER_DESC}}") every 15 minutes. Its
   service runs /usr/local/lib/.{{TIMER}}/run.sh, which copies the design files to /var/tmp/.pickup.
   The flag is in notes.txt next to the script.

4. Clean up. systemctl disable --now {{TIMER}}.timer, remove the .timer and .service files,
   systemctl daemon-reload; rm -f /etc/sudoers.d/{{SUDOF}}; usermod -L {{DEV}}.

For real: timers are as easy to hide as cron jobs, so check systemctl list-timers along with
/etc/cron.d; keep sudoers.d short and known; and use keys or strong unique passwords for ssh.
