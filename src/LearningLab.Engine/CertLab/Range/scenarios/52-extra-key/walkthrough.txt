The attacker is gone; their footholds are still on the box. You are cleaning up.

1. The way in. grep -Ei 'accepted|failed|new user' /var/log/secure shows the web account {{USER}} signed
   in from {{ATKIP}} after a failed try, then a "new user" line a few minutes later.

2. The extra administrator. cut -d: -f1,3,7 /etc/passwd lists the accounts; getent group wheel shows who
   can become root. {{ROGUE}} is there and you did not create it - that is the attacker's account. In its
   home is a dropped note, .note.txt, which holds the flag.

3. The hidden backdoor. A set-user-ID copy of a shell runs as its owner (root) for anyone, so it is a quiet
   way back to root. find / -perm -4000 -type f lists set-user-ID files; the odd one under /usr/local/bin
   (/usr/local/bin/{{BDNAME}}) is it.

4. Clean up before it is used. userdel -r {{ROGUE}} removes the account and its home; rm -f
   /usr/local/bin/{{BDNAME}} removes the backdoor. Leave the account and the attacker wires in permanent
   passwordless root with a sudoers drop-in.

For real: once someone is in, they add accounts, set-user-ID binaries, cron jobs and sudoers rules to stay.
Audit new users and group membership, scan for unexpected set-user-ID files, and keep the web account from
having a login shell or a weak password in the first place.
