1. Only one way in. nmap -sn 10.10.7.0/24 shows just jump01 (10.10.7.20) besides your
   box. The inside network 10.10.72.0/24 does not answer: your box has no route to it.

2. Foothold. jump01's FTP allows anonymous access; pub/handover.txt gives the pilot
   password. ssh pilot@10.10.7.20 for the jump-host flag.

3. See the second network. On jump01, ip addr shows a second address, 10.10.72.1:
   the jump host sits on both networks. From there, nmap -sn 10.10.72.0/24 finds
   store01 at 10.10.72.10, which your own box still cannot reach.

4. Pivot. The pilot password is reused on store01. From the jump host:
   ssh pilot@10.10.72.10 and read the inside flag. (A real pivot would also tunnel
   traffic through the jump host; here you run ssh from it directly.)

How to fix it: a jump host should not hand out credentials in an anonymous share, and
the same password should not open every box. Separate accounts and per-host keys stop
one foothold from becoming the whole inside network.
