The proxy sees everything that leaves the office, and size is a strong clue.

1. The odd one out. /var/log/proxy/access.log has one line per request. Ordinary browsing sends a few
   hundred bytes or kilobytes (bytes_out). One POST at {{TIME}} on 5 April sent {{BYTES}} bytes - hundreds
   of megabytes - from 10.10.25.40 to {{DEST}}, a file-sharing site nobody in the office uses.

2. Contain first. Add {{DEST}} to /etc/proxy/blocked-sites.txt: the proxy now refuses that site, so the
   next batch can't leave even before you find the cause. If you don't, the next night's batch goes out.

3. Find the cause. ssh itadmin@10.10.25.40, sudo -i. /etc/cron.d/{{SCRIPT}} runs /usr/local/bin/{{SCRIPT}}
   at 02:30 every night; it tars the client folder into /var/tmp/{{STAGE}} for sending. The flag is in
   manifest.txt there.

4. Clean up. rm -f /etc/cron.d/{{SCRIPT}} /usr/local/bin/{{SCRIPT}}.

For real: watch for uploads that are much larger than usual, especially at night and to file-sharing
sites; block categories of sites the business doesn't need; and find out how the job got onto the PC
(here, whoever could become root on ws40).
