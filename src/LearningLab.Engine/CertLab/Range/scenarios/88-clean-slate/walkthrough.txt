Wiping a log hides the details, but not the fact - and a forwarded copy keeps everything.

1. The clearing. On {{PC}}, Get-WinEvent -LogName Security shows almost nothing: event 1102, "the audit
   log was cleared", by {{VIC}}. Clearing the log always leaves that one event behind.

2. The copy. soc receives each event as it is written (/var/log/forwarded/{{LOWER:PC}}-security.log), so
   the clearing on the PC didn't touch it. It shows a failed and then a successful remote sign-in for
   {{VIC}} from {{ATKIP}} (Logon Type 10), the account {{ROGUE}} being created and added to
   Administrators, and the program that cleared the log: {{CLEARCMD}}.

3. Staying in. C:\ProgramData\Microsoft\Windows\Start Menu\Programs\StartUp\{{START}} runs for everyone
   who signs in and copies the plans share into C:\Users\Public\Libraries\cache. The flag is in
   readme.txt there.

4. Undo it. Remove-Item the start-up file and Remove-LocalUser {{ROGUE}}. If you are too slow, the next
   sign-in copies the plans out. (Also change {{VIC}}'s password.)

For real: forward logs to another machine as they are written (an intruder on one PC can't reach them),
alert on event 1102, and keep the Security log big enough to hold weeks of events.
