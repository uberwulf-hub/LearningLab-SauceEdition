# Lab: lab-01 | Finding your way with help | u01 | 25
Four small hunts with Get-Command, Get-Help, Get-Alias and Get-Member on WIN-SRV1. Store each answer in the variable the task names; the checks look at those variables.

@task t01 | Help | 10 | WIN-SRV1
Store in `$convertCmds` all commands whose **verb** is `ConvertTo` (use Get-Command), so that `$convertCmds.Name` includes ConvertTo-Csv and ConvertTo-Json.
@check WIN-SRV1 | $convertCmds holds the ConvertTo commands
$n = @($convertCmds.Name); $n -contains 'ConvertTo-Csv' -and $n -contains 'ConvertTo-Json' -and -not ($n -like 'Get-*')
@hint Get-Command -Verb ConvertTo
@solution WIN-SRV1
$convertCmds = Get-Command -Verb ConvertTo
$convertCmds
@explain
Get-Command filters by -Verb, -Noun, -Module and wildcard names. Because verbs come from a fixed list, asking by verb is a reliable way to discover related commands.
@endtask

@task t02 | Help | 10 | WIN-SRV1
Find out which cmdlet the alias `gm` stands for, and store its **full name** (as text) in `$gmCmd`.
@check WIN-SRV1 | $gmCmd is Get-Member
"$gmCmd" -eq 'Get-Member'
@hint Get-Alias gm shows "gm -> ...". Its Definition (or ResolvedCommandName) property holds the cmdlet's name.
@solution WIN-SRV1
Get-Alias gm
$gmCmd = (Get-Alias gm).Definition
@explain
Get-Alias NAME shows what an alias points to; Get-Alias -Definition CMDLET goes the other way and lists the aliases of a cmdlet.
@endtask

@task t03 | Help | 10 | WIN-SRV1
Using Get-Help, find the **position** of the `-Seconds` parameter of `Start-Sleep` (0 means it can be given without its name). Store the number in `$pos`.
@check WIN-SRV1 | $pos is 0
"$pos" -eq '0'
@check WIN-SRV1 | you looked it up with Get-Help
($CertLabHistory -join "`n") -match '(Get-Help|help)\s+Start-Sleep'
@hint Get-Help Start-Sleep -Parameter Seconds, or -Full.
@solution WIN-SRV1
Get-Help Start-Sleep -Parameter Seconds
$pos = 0
@explain
The parameter details (Required, Position, Accept pipeline input) appear with -Full or -Parameter. A positional parameter is what makes Start-Sleep 5 work without -Seconds.
@endtask

@task t04 | Help | 10 | WIN-SRV1
Use Get-Member on a string to find the method that tells whether a string **ends with** some text. Store the method's name in `$m`, then use it to store in `$isCsv` whether the text `'report.csv'` ends with `.csv`.
@check WIN-SRV1 | $m is EndsWith
"$m" -eq 'EndsWith'
@check WIN-SRV1 | $isCsv is True
$isCsv -eq $true
@hint 'x' | Get-Member -MemberType Method shows the string methods.
@solution WIN-SRV1
'x' | Get-Member -MemberType Method
$m = 'EndsWith'
$isCsv = 'report.csv'.EndsWith('.csv')
@explain
Get-Member lists everything an object can do. String methods are case-sensitive: 'REPORT.CSV'.EndsWith('.csv') is False, while 'REPORT.CSV' -like '*.csv' is True.
@endtask
