TOPIC
    about_Comparison_Operators

SHORT DESCRIPTION
    The operators that compare values and find text.

LONG DESCRIPTION
    Comparison operators start with a dash. They ignore upper and lower
    case; put a c in front for a case-sensitive version (-ceq, -clike,
    -cmatch) or an i to say explicitly that case is ignored.

    Equality
        -eq   equal                 -ne   not equal
        -gt   greater than          -ge   greater than or equal
        -lt   less than             -le   less than or equal

    Matching
        -like, -notlike     wildcards: * any text, ? one character,
                            [abc] one of these, [a-z] a range
        -match, -notmatch   regular expressions; a successful -match on a
                            single value fills $Matches

    Containment
        -contains, -notcontains   is the value in the collection on the left?
        -in, -notin               is the value on the left in the collection?

    Replacement
        -replace            replaces what a regular expression finds:
                            'a.b' -replace '\.', '-' gives a-b. In the
                            replacement, $1 and $2 are the groups (use single
                            quotes so PowerShell does not expand them).

    Type
        -is, -isnot         is the value of this type? 42 -is [int]

  The left side decides
    The value on the right is converted to the type of the value on the
    left: '10' -eq 10 is True, and 10 -eq '10.0' is True as well.

  A collection on the left filters
    With an array on the left, -eq, -ne, -gt, -like, -match and their
    friends return the items that match instead of True or False:

        1, 2, 3, 2 -eq 2      gives 2 and 2
        'a', 'b' -like 'a*'   gives a

    That is why a test for $null should put $null on the left:
    $null -eq $x answers the question even when $x is an array.

  $Matches
    After 'id=42' -match 'id=(\d+)', $Matches[0] is the whole match (id=42)
    and $Matches[1] the first group (42). Named groups (?<name>...) can be
    read as $Matches.name.
