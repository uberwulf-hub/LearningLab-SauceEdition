SYNOPSIS: Lists the background jobs of this session and shows how they are doing.
DESCRIPTION:
A background job runs a command while you keep using the console. Get-Job lists the jobs started in this session (by Start-Job, by Invoke-Command -AsJob and by other -AsJob commands), with their Id, Name, type, State and whether they have results waiting (HasMoreData).

The State is Running, Completed, Failed, Stopped or Blocked. HasMoreData is True until you collect the results with Receive-Job.

Jobs belong to the session that started them. Close the PowerShell window and its jobs, finished or not, are gone.
PARAMETER Id:
The job numbers to show. Position 0.
PARAMETER Name:
The job names to show. Wildcards work.
PARAMETER Job:
Job objects, for example from a variable. They can come through the pipeline.
PARAMETER State:
Shows only jobs in this state, for example -State Running or -State Failed.
PARAMETER IncludeChildJob:
Also lists each job's child jobs. Every job has at least one child that does the real work (one per computer for remote jobs).
EXAMPLE: Get-Job
Lists all jobs of this session.
EXAMPLE: $j = Start-Job { Start-Sleep 5; 'done' }; Get-Job -Id $j.Id
Shows one job; its State is Running until the five seconds have passed.
EXAMPLE: Get-Job -State Failed
Lists the jobs that failed. Receive-Job shows their errors.
EXAMPLE: Get-Job | Remove-Job
Removes every job (finished ones; running ones need -Force).
NOTES:
The alias is gjb.

Job Ids count up through the session, and each child job takes a number too, so your first two jobs are usually 1 and 3.

A completed job with HasMoreData True still holds its output: run Receive-Job to collect it.

Real 5.1 also has -Newest, -Before, -After, -HasMoreData and -ChildJobState; PowerShell 7 adds thread jobs (Start-ThreadJob) and the & operator at the end of a line to start a job.
RELATED: Start-Job, Receive-Job, Wait-Job, Stop-Job, Remove-Job, about_Jobs
