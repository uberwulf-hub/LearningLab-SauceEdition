TOPIC
    about_Signing

SHORT DESCRIPTION
    Adding a digital signature to scripts so others can check them.

LONG DESCRIPTION
    A signed script carries a signature block at its end. The signature
    says who signed it and proves the text has not changed since. The
    AllSigned and RemoteSigned execution policies use signatures to decide
    which scripts may run.

  What you need
    A code-signing certificate with its private key, in your certificate
    store. It can come from your organisation's certificate authority, a
    public one, or (for practice only) New-SelfSignedCertificate. Other
    computers trust the signature only if they trust the certificate that
    issued it, and AllSigned also asks whether to trust a new publisher.

        Get-ChildItem Cert:\CurrentUser\My -CodeSigningCert

  Signing a script
        $cert = Get-ChildItem Cert:\CurrentUser\My -CodeSigningCert |
            Select-Object -First 1
        Set-AuthenticodeSignature -FilePath C:\LabFiles\Tools.ps1 `
            -Certificate $cert -TimestampServer http://timestamp.example

    A time stamp keeps the signature valid after the certificate expires.

  Checking a signature
        Get-AuthenticodeSignature C:\LabFiles\Tools.ps1

    Status Valid means the text is unchanged and the issuer is trusted.
    HashMismatch means the script was edited after signing: sign it again.
    NotSigned and UnknownError (an untrusted issuer) are the other common
    answers.

  Things to know
    - Any change to the script, even one space, breaks the signature.
    - A self-signed certificate is for learning; it proves nothing to
      other people.
    - Signing does not make a script safe; it tells you who wrote it.
