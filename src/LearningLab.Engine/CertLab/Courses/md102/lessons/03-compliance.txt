# Unit: u03 | 3. Compliance policies | Compliance

A compliance policy is a list of rules a device must meet to count as healthy: encrypted disk, Secure Boot, a recent enough version of the operating system, antivirus running, not jailbroken. Intune checks each device against the policies assigned to it and marks it **Compliant** or **Not compliant**. On its own that is just a report; together with Conditional Access (next unit) it becomes a gate: only compliant devices reach company data. This unit covers the settings, what happens when a device fails, the tenant-wide compliance settings, mobile devices, monitoring, and Microsoft Defender for Endpoint's risk score.

## Lesson: l0301 | Compliance policies and their settings

Compliance policies are in **Devices > Compliance**. Each policy is for one platform. For **Windows 10 and later** the main groups of settings are:

- **Device Health** (from Windows' health attestation): Require BitLocker, Require Secure Boot, Require code integrity.
- **Device Properties**: minimum and maximum OS version (for example `10.0.26100`, Windows 11 24H2's build).
- **System Security**: require a password, minimum password length, firewall, TPM, antivirus, antispyware.
- **Microsoft Defender**: antimalware on, real-time protection, security intelligence up to date.
- **Microsoft Defender for Endpoint**: the highest machine risk score allowed (lesson 4).

A device must pass **every** policy assigned to it. Assign compliance policies to user groups or device groups; a policy assigned to a user group applies to that user's devices.

> A compliance policy only **checks**; it changes nothing on the device. To turn BitLocker on, the firewall on or Defender on, you use configuration and endpoint security policies (units 7, 12 and 13). A typical exam trap is a device that "stays noncompliant for BitLocker": the compliance policy can't encrypt it.

On a device you can look at the same things the policy checks:

```terminal
PS C:\Windows\system32> Confirm-SecureBootUEFI
True
PS C:\Windows\system32> Get-MpComputerStatus | Select-Object AMServiceEnabled, RealTimeProtectionEnabled, AntivirusSignatureAge

AMServiceEnabled RealTimeProtectionEnabled AntivirusSignatureAge
---------------- ------------------------- ---------------------
True             True                                          0


PS C:\Windows\system32> Get-BitLockerVolume -MountPoint C: | Select-Object MountPoint, VolumeStatus, ProtectionStatus

MountPoint VolumeStatus   ProtectionStatus
---------- ------------   ----------------
C:         FullyDecrypted Off
```

RB-LT-001's disk isn't encrypted yet, so a policy that requires BitLocker would mark it noncompliant.

@try
@task t1 | Compliance | 10 | portal
Create a Windows compliance policy called **Windows minimum** that requires Secure Boot, the firewall and at least OS version **10.0.26100**, assign it to **Windows devices**, then sync **RB-DT-010** and **RB-LT-001** and see which one passes.
@check portal | the policy exists with the three rules and the assignment
compliance["Windows minimum"].secureBoot == Require
compliance["Windows minimum"].firewall == Require
compliance["Windows minimum"].minOs == 10.0.26100
compliance["Windows minimum"].include has "Windows devices"
@check portal | the old Windows 10 desktop fails on its version, the laptop passes
device["RB-DT-010"].compliance == "Not compliant"
device["RB-DT-010"].reasons contains "older than the minimum"
device["RB-LT-001"].compliance == Compliant
@hint Platform Windows 10 and later; Minimum OS version is under Device Properties. Without a sync the devices are checked only at their next check-in.
@solution portal
create compliance name="Windows minimum" platform="Windows 10 and later" secureBoot=Require firewall=Require minOs=10.0.26100
assign compliance "Windows minimum" "Windows devices"
remote sync "RB-DT-010"
remote sync "RB-LT-001"
@endtask
@end

@quiz
? A compliance policy requires BitLocker. A laptop isn't encrypted. What does the compliance policy do?
- It turns BitLocker on at the next check-in
+ It marks the laptop as not compliant; encrypting it needs a disk encryption policy
- It wipes the laptop
! Compliance policies only evaluate. A disk encryption (endpoint security) policy turns BitLocker on.
? Two compliance policies are assigned to a device. It passes one and fails the other. What is its state?
- Compliant
+ Not compliant
- Compliant for the first policy only
! A device must pass every compliance policy assigned to it.
? Which value would you enter as a minimum OS version to require at least Windows 11 24H2?
+ 10.0.26100
- 11.0.24
- 24H2
! Windows 11 still reports version 10.0; 26100 is the 24H2 build number.
@end


## Lesson: l0302 | Actions for noncompliance and grace periods

Every compliance policy has **Actions for noncompliance**: what Intune does, and when, after a device fails. The actions you can choose include:

- **Mark device noncompliant**: always there. Its schedule (days after the device fails) is the **grace period**. With 0 days the device is noncompliant at once; with 3 days it shows **In grace period** first and becomes noncompliant three days later if nothing is fixed.
- **Send email to end user**: a message template (made under **Devices > Compliance > Notifications**) sent after N days, optionally copied to other addresses.
- **Send push notification** to the user (through the Company Portal app).
- **Remotely lock** the noncompliant device (mobile platforms).
- **Add device to retire list**: the device appears in a list from which an administrator can retire it; nothing is retired automatically.

> A device **in grace period** still counts as compliant for Conditional Access. That is the point of a grace period: the user keeps working while they fix the problem.

Grace periods are useful when a new rule arrives that people need time to meet (a new minimum OS version, for example), and when a device fails because of something users can fix themselves.

@try
@setup portal
create compliance name="Defender up to date" platform="Windows 10 and later" sigUpToDate=Require
assign compliance "Defender up to date" "Windows devices"
device RB-LT-002 sigAgeDays=9
@task t1 | Compliance | 10 | portal
The policy **Defender up to date** marks devices noncompliant at once. Priya has been travelling with little internet, so her Defender security intelligence is 9 days old; it will update once she is back in the office this week. Change the policy so that a failing device has **3 days** of grace before it is marked noncompliant and the user gets an email on the first day (day 0). Then sync **RB-LT-002** and look at its state.
@check portal | the policy has a 3-day grace period and an email
compliance["Defender up to date"].noncompliantDays == 3
compliance["Defender up to date"].emailDays == 0
@check portal | RB-LT-002 is in its grace period
device["RB-LT-002"].compliance == "In grace period"
@hint Edit the policy's Actions for noncompliance, then use Sync on the device.
@solution portal
set compliance "Defender up to date" noncompliantDays=3 emailDays=0
remote sync "RB-LT-002"
@endtask
@end

@quiz
? A policy marks devices noncompliant after 5 days. A device fails today. What does Conditional Access do with it today?
+ Treats it as compliant: it is in its grace period
- Blocks it at once
- Asks the user for MFA instead
! During the grace period the device counts as compliant.
? Which action for noncompliance removes the company data from a device automatically?
- Add device to retire list
- Send email to end user
+ None of them: the retire list only lists devices for an administrator to retire
! Retiring is a separate remote action an administrator starts; the retire list only collects candidates.
? Where do you create the message an email action sends?
- In Microsoft Entra ID's branding
+ In Devices > Compliance > Notifications (message templates)
- In the Company Portal's settings
! Email actions use notification message templates.
@end

## Lesson: l0303 | The tenant's compliance settings

Two settings apply to every device, in **Devices > Compliance > Compliance settings**:

- **Mark devices with no compliance policy assigned as**: Compliant (the default) or Not compliant. Secure tenants pick **Not compliant**, so a device that falls outside every policy can't pass a "require compliant device" rule in Conditional Access just because nobody checked it.
- **Compliance status validity period (days)**: a device that doesn't check in for that many days (30 by default) becomes noncompliant, because its last result is too old to trust.

There is also **Enhanced jailbreak detection** for iOS (uses location services). Intune shows these built-in checks as the **Default Device Compliance Policy** in a device's report: "Has a compliance policy assigned", "Is active" (checked in within the validity period) and "Enrolled user exists".

In Cert Lab these two values are in **Tenant administration > Settings**, section Compliance policy settings.

@try
@setup portal
create compliance name="Windows basics" platform="Windows 10 and later" secureBoot=Require
@task t1 | Compliance | 10 | portal
Make the tenant mark devices **without** a compliance policy as **Not compliant**, and shorten the compliance status validity period to **14** days. So that working laptops aren't hit, assign the existing policy **Windows basics** to **Windows devices**, then sync **RB-LT-001** and check it is still compliant.
@check portal | the tenant's compliance settings
tenant.noPolicyMark == "Not compliant"
tenant.validityDays == 14
@check portal | Windows devices have a policy, and RB-LT-001 stays compliant
compliance["Windows basics"].include has "Windows devices"
device["RB-LT-001"].compliance == Compliant
@hint Tenant administration > Settings, section Compliance policy settings; then assign the policy and sync.
@solution portal
tenant noPolicyMark="Not compliant" validityDays=14
assign compliance "Windows basics" "Windows devices"
remote sync "RB-LT-001"
@endtask
@end

@quiz
? A device has no compliance policy and the tenant setting is the default. A Conditional Access policy requires a compliant device. What happens?
+ The device is allowed: with the default setting it is marked compliant
- The device is blocked: it has no policy
- The user is asked to enrol again
! The default for "Mark devices with no compliance policy assigned as" is Compliant, which is why secure tenants change it.
? A laptop has been switched off for 45 days with the default settings. What is its compliance state when it is checked?
- Compliant, from its last check-in
+ Not compliant: it hasn't checked in within the validity period
- In grace period
! After the validity period (30 days by default) without a check-in, the device is marked not compliant.
? Where does a device's report show "Has a compliance policy assigned" and "Is active"?
- In each custom compliance policy
+ In the Default Device Compliance Policy
- In the Conditional Access sign-in log
! These built-in checks appear as the Default Device Compliance Policy.
@end

## Lesson: l0304 | Phones, monitoring and Defender for Endpoint's risk score

### iOS/iPadOS and Android settings

Phones have their own compliance settings. The ones to know:

- **iOS/iPadOS**: block **jailbroken** devices, minimum and maximum OS version, require a passcode (with a minimum length), and the maximum allowed device threat level from a mobile threat defence service.
- **Android Enterprise**: block **rooted** devices, require a passcode, minimum OS version, and the **Play Integrity** verdict (basic integrity, or basic integrity and device integrity), which detects tampered or unofficial Android builds.

### Monitoring

- **Devices > Monitor > Compliance** and **Devices > Compliance**: how many devices are compliant, noncompliant, in grace period or not evaluated.
- Each policy's **device status** and **per-setting status**: which rule fails on how many devices.
- A device's own **Device compliance** page lists every policy and the reason it failed. Cert Lab shows these reasons on the device's page.
- A device that is **Not evaluated** hasn't been checked yet (just enrolled, or no check-in since the policy was assigned).

### Microsoft Defender for Endpoint's machine risk score

Defender for Endpoint gives each onboarded Windows device a **risk score**: Clear, Low, Medium or High, based on active threats found on it. A compliance setting, *Require the device to be at or under the machine risk score*, turns that into compliance. For it to work:

1. Connect Intune and Defender for Endpoint (the **Microsoft Defender for Endpoint** connector in Endpoint security, turned on from both sides).
2. Onboard the devices to Defender for Endpoint, usually with an endpoint detection and response (EDR) policy in Intune.
3. Set the highest allowed risk in a compliance policy.

A device that isn't onboarded has no risk score, so it fails that rule. Unit 12 practises the connector and onboarding.

@try
@setup portal
device "Priya's Pixel" rooted=Yes
@task t1 | Compliance | 10 | portal
Priya's Android phone has been rooted. Create an Android Enterprise compliance policy called **Android basics** that blocks rooted devices and requires a passcode, assign it to **Sales**, sync **Priya's Pixel** and check that it is noncompliant because it is rooted.
@check portal | the policy blocks rooted devices and is assigned to Sales
compliance["Android basics"].rooted == Block
compliance["Android basics"].password == Require
compliance["Android basics"].include has Sales
@check portal | Priya's phone fails because it is rooted
device["Priya's Pixel"].compliance == "Not compliant"
device["Priya's Pixel"].reasons contains rooted
@hint Platform Android Enterprise; Rooted devices is under Device Health. Sync the phone afterwards.
@solution portal
create compliance name="Android basics" platform="Android Enterprise" rooted=Block password=Require
assign compliance "Android basics" Sales
remote sync "Priya's Pixel"
@endtask
@end

@quiz
? A compliance policy allows at most a Low machine risk score. A Windows laptop isn't onboarded to Defender for Endpoint. What is its state?
- Compliant: no score means no risk
+ Not compliant: it has no risk score to compare
- Not evaluated forever
! The rule needs a risk score from Defender for Endpoint; a device that isn't onboarded fails it.
? Which Android setting detects devices running a tampered or unofficial build?
- Block rooted devices only
+ The Play Integrity verdict
- Minimum OS version
! Play Integrity checks basic integrity and device integrity.
? A newly enrolled device shows "Not evaluated". What does that mean?
+ Intune hasn't checked it against its compliance policies yet
- The device is blocked by Conditional Access
- The device has no user
! Not evaluated is the state before the first compliance check.
@end
