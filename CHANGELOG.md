# Changelog

What changes in each version, in plain words. Newest first.

## [Unreleased]

## [0.5.0] - 2026-11-19

### Changed

- Spelling Bee now comes with five word lists of its own (Easy to Hardest Words). The school study lists are no longer built in: add your school's list on each device with Add a word list, and it comes back just as before.

### Added

- Network Lab, from App Hub: build networks with PCs, laptops, phones, servers, printers, switches, routers, Wi-Fi and the internet, cable them with a tap, and watch the link lights. Tap a device to set it up: routers and switches have a real-style command line (? for help, Tab to finish a word), computers have their Command Prompt, PowerShell or Terminal and their IP and Wi-Fi settings, and the wireless router has its setup page. Simulation mode stops time so you can watch each message travel and look inside it. The labs tell a short story and tick off the goals as you reach them, with hints, the answer and "Do it for me". Drag to move around, pinch to zoom; on a phone everything fits the screen. Your network is kept for next time, and .netlab files from App Hub open here too.
- Network Lab: each computer, phone and gadget now has its own pretend screen. Windows-like, Ubuntu-like and macOS-like desktops (Settings, Control Panel, File Explorer, Command Prompt), phones and tablets, a printer's front panel with real-style buttons, and a TV's settings. Open web pages from your own servers in the pretend web browser, add a network printer and print a test page, and join Wi-Fi with a password. Servers have a Services tab (web pages, DNS names, DHCP and shared folders), and routers and switches have Config forms that type the commands for you and show them. On a phone each pretend window fills the screen, with a big close button.
- Network Lab's Physical view: see where everything is on a house, a small office, a school or your own blank floor plan, with rooms, walls, doors, racks, Wi-Fi range and cables running along the walls (their lengths follow the plan). Add rooms, walls, doors, labels and racks with a tap or a drag, put a picture of a real plan underneath, and pick the exact port on a drawing of each device's front panel when cabling.
- Drawing in Network Lab: rectangles, circles, lines, arrows, freehand, text, sticky notes, areas, pictures and labels on devices and cables, plus a library of buildings, roads, furniture, trees and more, on both views. Resize and turn with your finger, change colours and text, group, line up, copy and paste; add a street, park or island map underneath, set the map's scale, and show or lock each layer.
- Cert Lab, from App Hub: study for RHCSA, PowerShell, MD-102, AZ-800 and Security+ with lessons (read aloud in the device's voice, with Copy buttons for commands), flash cards, cheat sheets, search, practice machines to type on, labs that check your work, and practice exams with a timer and a full report afterwards. The terminal works with a phone's keyboard and has a key row for Tab, Ctrl+C, the arrows and copy / paste; the boot menu, vi and nano work by touch too. An exam or lab in progress is kept when you leave and offered back next time. Everything is simulated: nothing reaches a real network.
- Cert Lab's practice portal and consoles: for the MD-102 course, a pretend Intune admin portal and its devices (make groups, compliance and configuration policies, assign them, sync a device from its pretend Settings app, try a sign-in with What If); for the AZ-800 course, pretend Windows Server consoles (Users and Computers, Group Policy and its editor, DNS, Windows Admin Center, the Azure portal) beside each server's PowerShell. Everything is simulated; on a phone the menus fold away and lists become cards.
- Cyber Range, from App Hub: capture-the-flag practice on pretend networks. Break in as Red Team, stay quiet past a defender, defend as Blue Team or a SOC analyst, solve CTF puzzles, the web shop lab and the Linux Ladder, from Beginner to Expert. Hints stay hidden until you ask, Beginner challenges explain themselves in plain words and every dotted word can be tapped, and ranks, badges and streaks track your progress. A challenge in progress is kept when you leave and offered back next time. Everything is pretend: nothing reaches a real network.

## [0.4.0] - 2026-11-14

### Added

- Scratch paper in math practice: write and work the problem out right under it with a finger, a pen or the mouse (three colours, an eraser, Undo and Clear). It wipes itself for each new problem, and can be hidden.
- Spanish cartoons: 12 short, silly animated cartoons (about half a minute each, three per level) with a frog, a robot, a cat, a dog, a hen and a cactus talking in Spanish, each in their own voice. Watch, work out what's going on, then answer "What's going on?" and a couple more questions. Captions in Spanish or Spanish and English if you need them, a slower speed, and the script afterwards with every word tappable. In the car, cartoons play twice and then ask what happened.
- Soft, gentle sounds for right and wrong answers in Languages (lessons, flashcards, quizzes, stories and the verb trainer), a faint tick on Next and Continue, and a short buzz on phones for each.

### Fixed

- On Windows, the conversation lines in a lesson (and other phrases) could stop being read aloud after tapping a few speaker buttons.

## [0.3.0] - 2026-11-12

### Added

- A math skill map: every 2nd and 6th grade skill, grouped like school (numbers, adding and subtracting, fractions, ratios, geometry...), with each player's score from 0 to 100: Getting started, Building, Strong, the Challenge zone, and Mastered at 100. The score climbs quickly at first and slowly near the top, and the questions get harder as it climbs. Other grades are coming.
- Math practice that teaches: miss a question and you see "Not quite", the right answer, and a step-by-step explanation of that exact question with its own numbers. Tap "Got it" to carry on. Reaching 100 celebrates with bonus reward points.
- Up next: the Math page and the skill map suggest what to practice, starting with skills you've begun and then the next new ones.
- Spelling Bee practice: hear a word (or hear it slowly), spell it out loud or type it, and find out right away. Hints show the blanks, then the first and last letters. A first miss gets one more try; after that the word is shown with the wrong letters marked and spelled out loud for you. Words you miss go into My Tricky Words to practice again, and leave once you spell them right the first time. Each player keeps their own lists, settings and scores.
- The 2026-2027 school spelling bee study lists (Starter Words, Two Bee, Three Bee, Advanced, Expert) come with Spelling Bee, and you can add your own lists from a file or by pasting words.
- Spanish lessons: 71 short lessons (about 10 minutes each) that follow the units. Meet the new phrases, hear a conversation line by line (tap for the English), practise with eight kinds of exercise (pick the answer, listen, fill the gap, put words in order, type it, match pairs, say it, choose a reply), read a tip, and see how you did. Anything you get wrong comes back once at the end, and the right answer is always said out loud.
- A daily goal: points for lessons, flashcards, quizzes, stories and the verb trainer, a ring that fills up through the day, your streak, and a big Continue button for the next lesson. Pick 10, 20, 30 or 50 points a day in Settings.
- The verb trainer: present, past (preterite) and near future (ir a) for 83 everyday verbs, typed or picked from four. It shows the whole verb afterwards and points out which forms are irregular. The answers are worked out by the app itself, so they're always consistent.
- Stories: 20 short stories in four levels, read aloud a sentence at a time or all at once. Tap any word for its meaning, tap a line for its English, and answer a few questions at the end. New stories open as you reach their unit.
- In the car: a "Conversation drill" asks "How would you say...?", gives you time to say it, then the answer; long phrases are built up from the end, and earlier phrases come back to check they stuck. Stories can be listened to in the car too.
- Say it: on phones and computers that can recognise Spanish speech without the internet, the app listens to what you say and shows which words it heard. Otherwise you say it out loud, listen, and mark yourself. Nothing you say is recorded or sent anywhere.

### Changed

- The Spelling Bee is inside Language Arts > English.
- The Android app is now built and signed on GitHub, like the other SauceStore apps, with a new key: if Learning Lab is already on a phone, uninstall it once, then install this version from SauceStore. (Windows isn't affected.)
- The home page is now laid out by school subject: Language Arts, Math, Science, Social Studies, World Languages, and Computers & Technology. Tap a subject to see what's inside; things that keep your progress ask who's playing first. All of Learning Games (with family games and the parent view) is still there at the bottom of the home page.

### Fixed

- Emulator Lab no longer closes when you open the Look inside tab a second time.

## [0.2.0] - 2026-11-11

### Added

- Languages, starting with Spanish (as spoken in Mexico and the US Southwest): 24 units from hello to short conversations, flashcards that bring words back just before you'd forget them, quizzes by ear and by sight, and spoken lessons to listen to in the car (they keep playing with the phone's screen off, and can be saved as an audio file).
- Voices: pick the Spanish and English voices, with steps to add your device's free Spanish voices. On Windows, Learning Games' Spanish now finds those voices too.
- Emulator Lab has moved over from App Hub: a small computer made of software to learn how computers work, with its own operating system, games like Snake, an assembler to write your own programs, and a look inside the processor as it runs. It works on phones too, with a row of extra keys (Esc, Ctrl, arrows, F keys). On Windows, the first start brings across App Hub's Emulator Lab settings, the program you were writing and the files on its disk (App Hub's copy is left as it is).

## [0.1.0] - 2026-11-10

### Added

- Learning Lab (Sauce Edition) is a new app for Windows and Android that brings App Hub's learning apps together. The
  first one is Learning Games: the math games (Asteroid Blaster, Number Line Frog, Pizza Shop, Math Race, Boss Battle),
  practising any 2nd- or 6th-grade skill with pictures, the Long Division Workshop, My Build and My Lab, the progress
  report, and Quiz Quest, Flash Cards, Lightning Round and Memory Match in history, English, US citizenship, Spanish,
  coding and science, with spaced review.
- On Windows, the first start brings across App Hub's Learning Games players and their progress (App Hub's copy is left
  as it is). The block games open in their own window as before.
- Family games on the home network work between phones, Learning Lab on a PC, and App Hub's Learning Games.
- Emulator Lab, Network Lab and Cert Lab show "Coming soon" until they move over.
